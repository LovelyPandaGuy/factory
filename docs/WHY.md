# Why we built it, and who it is for

Africa buys most of its software from somewhere else. We want it built here, by young
Africans, to a standard nobody has to apologise for. Africa as builder, not buyer.

South Africa has the people. Millions of young South Africans have no work, and many of
them can already code. What they usually lack is what gets a junior
trusted with real work: a senior engineer who checks everything before it ships.

The factory is that check. It does not think for you. It makes sure what you and your AI
built is what was asked for, proves it, and keeps the evidence. That evidence is what
lets a small team of young engineers sell finished work to a company that would
otherwise buy it from overseas, and what lets a junior show an employer proof instead of
promises.

## How it serves our four pillars

| Pillar | What the factory does for it |
|---|---|
| **AI and Innovation** | Puts AI to real work under rules, with proof. You learn to direct and check an AI, which is the skill employers now pay for. |
| **Entrepreneurship and Intrapreneurship** | A small team can quote a fixed price in rand for a finished piece of work and hand over a report the client can check. Inside a company, the same report gets your change approved. |
| **Wealth Creation** | The tool is MIT licensed and the lessons are open. The method belongs to whoever uses it, and a team's lessons, checks and record of delivered work stay with that team. |
| **Think-Tank** | Every file in [`lessons/`](../lessons/) is a dated record of a real failure, marked when it is South Africa specific. Together they are public research on how software goes wrong here, and how to catch it. |

The pillars are on [nextgencollective.co.za](https://nextgencollective.co.za/#pillars).

## Using it well in South Africa

**Find yourself in this list.**

- **A student or graduate looking for work.** Use it on your own projects. Every job
  leaves a spec, test results, screenshots and an independent review in the spec's
  folder. Put that in your portfolio: it shows you ship proven work, not code that ran
  once on your laptop.
- **A freelancer or small studio.** Price the finished piece, not the hours. Give the
  client the page `factory report` writes. Read [the limits](WHAT-IT-CANNOT-DO.md) before
  you promise anything.
- **An engineer in a company.** Run the first pass on your team's project. It writes
  nothing. Take the one real problem it finds to your lead, fixed.
- **A company or programme taking on juniors.** Let juniors take real jobs with the checks
  and the independent review in place. You read the report and the change, not every
  line from scratch.

**Five rules.**

1. **Read every change before you commit it.** The factory proves; you decide. If you
   cannot explain the change, do not ship it. You learn nothing, and the client pays for
   that.
2. **Personal data falls under POPIA.** Never put a real person's name, ID number or phone
   number in a spec, a test or a prompt. Invent them. Your AI tool sends what it reads to
   servers outside South Africa.
3. **Never call work "bug-free", "secure" or "compliant".** Say what passed, what was
   flagged and what was not checked. That honesty is the whole reputation.
4. **Write the lesson.** When something goes wrong, `factory lesson new` takes ten minutes.
   Mark it South Africa specific when it is. The next person avoids it, and the good ones
   become checks.
5. **Know your cost.** The factory is free. Your AI tool is not, and a job keeps it busy
   for several minutes. Run a few small jobs and know what one costs you before you quote
   anyone.

**What it will not do.** It does not create jobs by itself, and it does not replace
learning to code. It makes the work of a young engineer who can code checkable, so it
can be trusted, paid for and built on.
